Front-Office Insurance & Billing Workflow

A sample end-to-end process for a medical or dental practice, from verifying coverage to billing the patient.

Step 1Verify Insurance
Step 2Manage Appointments
Step 3Submit Claim
Step 4Bill Patient

1. Verify Insurance · Before the visit

  • Collect the patient's name, date of birth, insurance card (front and back), and subscriber details if different.
  • Check eligibility through the payer portal or by phone.
  • Confirm active coverage on the date of service and identify the plan type.
  • Record deductible and amount met, annual maximum and amount used, coverage percentage by service category, copays, frequency limits, and waiting periods.
  • Check prior authorization requirements for planned procedures.
  • Estimate expected insurance payment and the patient's portion.
  • Document the date, method, representative name, and reference number in the patient record.
  • Flag inactive coverage, limits, or an expected balance early, before the visit.

2. Schedule, Reschedule & Cancel Appointments · Calendar management

Schedule

  • Confirm patient identity and reason for visit.
  • Match the procedure to the appointment length and provider.
  • Check the calendar and book the slot.
  • Confirm insurance verification is complete.
  • Send confirmation and set reminders.

Reschedule

  • Locate the existing appointment and offer available times with the same provider.
  • Update the calendar and release the old slot.
  • Send an updated confirmation.
  • Check that the change does not affect authorization or benefits timing.

Cancel

  • Confirm identity and appointment; record the reason.
  • Check the cancellation policy and notice period.
  • Free the slot and offer it to the waitlist.
  • Offer to rebook before ending the call.

3. Submit a Claim · After the visit

  • Confirm treatment is complete and documented in the visit notes.
  • Match services to the correct procedure and diagnosis codes (for example, CDT for dental, CPT and ICD-10 for medical).
  • Compare the claim against verified coverage, limits, and frequency rules.
  • Attach supporting documents such as x-rays, clinical notes, narratives, or prior authorization numbers.
  • Check patient and subscriber information, provider IDs, payer ID, dates, and amounts.
  • Submit electronically through the payer portal or clearinghouse, and save the confirmation.
  • Track pending claims within the payer's expected processing time.
  • Correct and resubmit rejections, or start an appeal for denials.

4. Bill the Patient · After the payer responds

  • Review the EOB or remittance when the payer responds.
  • Post payments and adjustments accurately to the patient account.
  • Calculate the patient portion: deductible, coinsurance, copay, and non-covered services.
  • Confirm the amount matches the EOB and the verified estimate before billing.
  • Send a clear statement explaining charges and insurance payments in plain language.
  • Offer payment options, including payment plans, according to practice policy.
  • Record payments and follow up on unpaid balances on a set schedule.
  • Refund overpayments and correct account errors.
Quality & compliance throughout: Double-check codes, dates, and amounts; share information only with verified, authorized parties; keep documentation clear enough for another team member to continue the work; and track open items until resolved.

This is a general illustration of a common workflow. It contains no employer-specific procedures, systems, patient data, or confidential information. Actual processes vary by practice, payer, and plan.
